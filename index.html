This project addresses the challenge of precise urban geolocalization by predicting the exact coordinates of images captured across the Ben-Gurion University campus. By utilizing a multi-task training framework built upon a pre-trained DINOv2 foundation model, we achieve high-precision results even in environments with unreliable GPS or significant illumination changes.

Methodology

Our research cycle involved managing the entire lifecycle of a Deep Learning product, from data acquisition to model optimization:

  • Data Collection & Manual Geotagging: We collected approximately 1,500 images across campus. Because standard smartphone GPS is noisy, we performed manual geotagging to create accurate ground truth labels.
  • Multi-Task Training: To stabilize predictions on a small dataset, we trained the model on two simultaneous tasks: coordinate regression (Latitude/Longitude) and zone classification (25 topological clusters).
  • Progressive Unfreezing: We started by freezing layers 1-11 of the pre-trained DINOv2 backbone. Every 20 training epochs, we unfroze an additional layer to progressively adapt the model to campus-specific features.

Model Architecture

The system uses a shared DINOv2 backbone to extract features that are passed through a shared block followed by separate regression and classification heads. The classification head acts as a regularizer, helping the model learn a general sense of location before refining exact coordinates through the regression head.

Figure 2: Architecture overview featuring DINOv2 backbone with dual heads.

Training Analysis

During training, we synchronized our progressive unfreezing schedule with updates to the learning rate scheduler. This strategy allowed the model to converge effectively, reaching a stable "Victory Lap" phase with a best mean error of 12.48m and a median of 8.97m.

Figure 3: Localization error (meters) and Learning Rate over training epochs.

Campus Geolocalization Analysis

The spatial distribution of our results shows that while the DINOv2 backbone provides solid accuracy for both day and night photos, outliers still occur. These failures are often linked to regions with low image detail, repetitive textures, or GPS drift in the original data.
